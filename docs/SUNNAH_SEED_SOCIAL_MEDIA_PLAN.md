# Sunnah Seed — 90-Day Social Media Growth Plan

**Prepared:** 30 September 2026  
**Role:** Senior Social Media Analyst / Islamic children’s-media strategy  
**Primary market:** English-speaking Muslim parents and caregivers; children experience the content with adult guidance.  
**Core age:** 4–8 at launch; adapt selected parent resources for 9–12 later.

> **The essential recommendation:** before investing in growth, make Sunnah Seed visibly trustworthy and recognisable everywhere: one name, one parent-facing promise, one source-review process, and a sustainable stream of useful short videos. The immediate public impression must change from “an account waiting to start” to “a gentle, reliable place where families practise one beautiful Sunnah at a time.”

A beautiful logo without this system will not build a durable Islamic children’s brand.

## 2. Brand foundation

### 2.1 Positioning

**Parent-facing promise**

> *Sunnah Seed helps Muslim families grow gentle daily habits through short, joyful, source-checked Sunnah lessons for little hearts and their grown-ups.*

**What makes it different**

1. **One small practice, not an overwhelming lecture.** Each episode ends with an achievable family action: say *Bismillah*, return a greeting, share, tidy, make a du‘a, or show kindness.
2. **Children enjoy it; adults trust it.** Child-simple language is paired with a visible source reference and a parent note.
3. **Character before performance.** Content nurtures mercy, honesty, gratitude, cleanliness, courage, adab, and love of Allah—not comparison, shame, or “perfect Muslim child” pressure.
4. **An actual seed-to-growth journey.** The visual metaphor gives every post a coherent structure: *Seed* (learn), *Water* (practise), *Bloom* (reflect/share with a caregiver).

### 2.2 Audience model

| Audience | Need | Message | Best surfaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: parents/caregivers, 25–44** | Safe, concise, authentic Islamic support that fits busy routines. | “Try one meaningful Sunnah together today.” | Instagram carousels/Reels, Facebook posts, YouTube descriptions, email/free printable. |
| **Core learners: children 4–8** | Repetition, warmth, concrete examples, clear actions, visual cues. | “Let’s grow a good habit together.” | Adult-supervised YouTube videos, reels viewed with a caregiver, printables. |
| **Extension: children 9–12** | More independence, reasons behind practices, respectful challenge. | “Understand it, then make it yours.” | Separate parent-approved playlists, quizzes/worksheets, longer story episodes. |
| **Amplifiers: teachers, madrasahs, masjid family teams, Muslim parenting creators** | Reliable material they can share without spending hours checking it. | “A source-checked family resource for this week’s lesson.” | Facebook, Instagram, YouTube playlists, partner toolkit. |

**Important operating principle:** social accounts are usually adult accounts. Write public calls to action to **parents and caregivers**, never ask children to DM, submit personal details, reveal their school/location, or use a platform account.

### 2.3 Brand voice and visual direction

| Use | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | Warm, hopeful, clear, calm, encouraging: “Let’s try,” “Allah loves…,” “Ask your grown-up.” | Guilt, fear-led motivation, debate, sarcasm, sectarian language, or babyish speech. |
| **Language** | One learning point per post; plain English; introduce Arabic slowly with transliteration, meaning, and accurate pronunciation support. | Long text blocks, unexplained Arabic, internet slang that dates quickly, or sweeping religious claims. |
| **Visual system** | Soft seed/leaf motif, generous whitespace, high contrast text, one consistent type family, captions on every video, nature/home/routine objects. | Visual clutter, flashing edits, horror/fear imagery, meme templates, or clickbait facial expressions. |
| **Sacred respect** | Treat Qur’anic text and Allah’s names with dignity; have a safe display and archival policy. | Decorative misuse, placing sacred text in transient/low-res assets, or using Qur’an recitation as generic background sound. |

**Recommended palette:** deep leaf green `#1F5A45`, warm saffron `#E9B44C`, sky `#DFF3F2`, cream `#FFF9EE`, and charcoal `#24302C`. This is a direction, not a mandatory logo redesign.

## 3. Islamic integrity and child-safety charter

This is the asset that makes the brand credible. Publish a shortened version on the link-in-bio page and follow the full version internally.

### 3.1 Source-review standard

1. **Every teaching claim has a source before scripting.** Record Qur’an as `Surah:ayah`; record hadith collection, number, grading, and translation source. Do not cite a social-media graphic as a source.
2. **Distinguish categories.** Label Qur’an, authentic/hasan hadith, scholarly explanation, customary advice, and creative story elements clearly. Never make a weak narration sound certain.
3. **Use a qualified reviewer.** A named, suitably qualified Islamic studies reviewer signs off on scripts, captions, transliterations, and any answer that goes beyond simple agreed practice. The reviewer’s methodology and school/approach should be stated transparently, without turning every children’s lesson into a debate.
4. **Handle legitimate difference with adab.** Where established scholarly views differ, either choose a broadly agreed topic for children or say, “Families may learn this differently; ask your trusted teacher.” Do not declare other Muslims wrong in children’s content.
5. **No health, financial, or legal rulings.** Sunnah Seed is educational media—not a substitute for a doctor, safeguarding professional, local scholar, or legal advice. Avoid medical cure claims, including around foods or remedies.
6. **Correction protocol.** If a mistake is confirmed: pause/replace the asset, acknowledge the correction plainly where it was published, update the source log, and explain the corrected learning point without defensiveness.

A useful character anchor is the authentic teaching, “The best amongst you are those who have the best manners and character” (Sahih al-Bukhari 3559). It should shape the tone, not be used as a slogan without context.

### 3.2 Sensitive visual and audio policy

The team should have an advisory decision before production, not after criticism.

- Do **not** depict Allah, the Prophets عليهم السلام, angels, Jannah, Jahannam, jinn, or the unseen as visual characters. Tell sacred stories through objects, landscapes, maps, respectful text, voice, and parent-supported activities.
- Choose an explicit illustration policy with the adviser, because Muslim families hold differing views on animate imagery. If using people/animal characters, make the policy transparent and provide an alternative object/nature-led style where possible.
- Avoid fear, violence, disturbing descriptions, mockery, and miracle scenes rendered as spectacle.
- Use licensed, original, or properly cleared assets only. Decide and document a music/sound-effects approach with the reviewer; a conservative, inclusive launch option is warm voice, natural ambience, and permissible non-musical sound design as advised.
- Have Arabic/Qur’anic text reviewed character by character. Do not ask a child to repeat something they cannot read safely from a fast-moving reel.

### 3.3 Child safeguarding and privacy

- Treat content that is directed to children as such in YouTube’s audience settings. YouTube says creators must set an audience, and “made for kids” content loses features including comments, notifications, cards/end screens, and personalised advertising. Build calls to action around a **parent resource link and verbal parent prompt**, not comments or the notification bell.
- Do not collect a child’s name, photograph, voice, email, school, location, or “tell us your age” response. Any resource sign-up is parent-only and should collect the minimum necessary information.
- Do not invite children into DMs, group chats, live video calls, or open challenges. Route enquiries to a parent-facing contact channel.
- Never show identifiable children unless there is documented, specific parent/guardian consent, a clear usage scope, secure records, and a safeguarding review. The safer launch choice is to use staff, animation/object-led visuals, and stock assets with appropriate licences.
- Apply keyword filters; check all comments/messages daily on adult-facing posts; hide/delete personal information, bullying, religious abuse, scams, and unsafe requests. Escalate credible safeguarding concerns via a written internal policy.
- Separate **children’s viewing content** from **parent education** where useful. A “For Parents” playlist/post series can explain references, routines, and practical support without asking children to interact.

This plan is operational guidance, not legal advice. Obtain local legal advice for the countries served and review current platform policy before launch or paid promotion.

## 4. Content architecture

### 4.1 The five repeatable series

| Series | Format and length | Parent/child value | Example episode | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **One Sunnah Today** | Vertical video, 15–30 seconds | One visible habit to try immediately. | “Before we eat: we begin with *Bismillah*.” | 2 per week |
| **Little Du‘a, Big Heart** | Vertical video, 15–25 seconds + parent card | Gentle repetition with context and source checked by reviewer. | “A bedtime du‘a: make it part of a calm family routine.” | 1 per week |
| **Story Seed** | YouTube 3–5 minutes; 2 short clips | Seerah/character/storytelling without sacred depictions. | “How sharing can make a room feel happier.” | 1 episode per week |
| **Grow Good Character** | Carousel, 5–7 slides; simple reel version | Gives adults a conversation prompt and children a practice. | “Three kind words to try at home today.” | 1 per week |
| **The Parent Patch** | Carousel, voiceover, Story, or Facebook post | Source notes, developmental guidance, activity/printable. | “How to introduce a new Sunnah without turning it into a battle.” | 1 per week |

**Recommended mix:** 70% practical evergreen learning, 20% relationship/community/behind-the-scenes trust, 10% timely seasonal content. Do not chase every trend; only use a trend if it is respectful, accessible, and serves the lesson.

### 4.2 The Sunnah Seed episode formula

Every short-form video follows the same memorable pattern:

1. **Hook (0–2 seconds):** “A tiny Sunnah for snack time…”
2. **Seed (2–8 seconds):** one clear lesson, shown in an everyday family setting.
3. **Why it matters (8–15 seconds):** one warm character connection: gratitude, kindness, cleanliness, care.
4. **Try it (final 5 seconds):** “Can you try it with your grown-up today?”
5. **Trust layer:** on-screen `Sources in caption • Parent guide in bio`; full citation in caption/description.

For a 3–5 minute Story Seed: open with a question, tell one simple conflict/choice, explain the character lesson, give one home activity, and finish with a parent prompt. Do not over-pack a lesson with five rulings.

### 4.3 Caption and source template

```text
🌱 TODAY’S SEED: [one short lesson]

Try it together: [one concrete action].
Ask your child: “[open question]”

For parents: [one 1–2 sentence age-appropriate note].
Source: [Qur’an / hadith collection + number + grading / reviewed explanation].

Save this for your next [meal/bedtime/family routine].
#SunnahSeed #[specific series tag] #[limited relevant discovery tag]
```

Keep hashtags intentional: use the branded `#SunnahSeed`, the series tag, and 2–4 genuine discovery terms. Do not put a large cloud of unrelated hashtags under children’s content.

### 4.4 Month-one editorial calendar

All exact wording, Arabic, citations, and activity instructions are placeholders until reviewer approval. The point is a connected family journey, not a list of unverified “facts.”

| Week | Long YouTube / Story Seed | Three short-form pieces | Parent/carousel piece | Community prompt to adults |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Plant the seed** | Welcome: “What is a Sunnah habit?” | Welcome / saying salaam / before-eating routine | “How to use Sunnah Seed in 5 gentle minutes” | “Which routine is hardest in your family day?” |
| **2 — Kind hands, kind words** | A story about kindness and sharing | A kind phrase / helping at home / gratitude du‘a | “Praise effort, not perfection” | “Tag a caregiver who makes little habits feel possible.” |
| **3 — Calm routines** | Bedtime routine: gentle preparation and reflection | Tidy together / bedtime transition / reviewed du‘a clip | “A 3-step calm bedtime cue” | “Save this for tonight.” |
| **4 — Good company and gratitude** | A child-friendly story of good character | Greeting someone / sharing / thank-you practice | “Questions that build reflection instead of memorisation” | “Which Seed should we water next month?” |

**Launch-library minimum before promotion:** 1 welcome video, 4 Story Seeds, 12 short videos, 4 parent guides, 3 reusable Story templates, and one downloadable starter sheet. This prevents a new visitor from seeing an empty or inconsistent page.

## 5. Platform playbooks

### 5.1 YouTube — the searchable learning library

**Purpose:** build trust and discoverability through lasting videos; make Shorts feed the deeper library.

**Set up before the first upload**

- Banner: `Gentle Sunnah habits for little hearts & their grown-ups` plus the schedule, e.g. `New Seeds every Tuesday & Friday`.
- Trailer (30–45 seconds): mission, age range, source promise, parent guidance, and what to watch first.
- Organise public playlists from day one: **Start Here**, **One Sunnah Today**, **Little Du‘as**, **Story Seeds**, and **For Parents**.
- Add channel keywords in natural language: `Islamic learning for children`, `Sunnah for kids`, `Muslim family routines`, `good character for children`—only where accurate.
- Create a thumbnail template: one friendly object/scene, 3–5 large words, consistent colour band, no visual depictions of sacred figures.
- Add a source block and a parent-facing contact/safety line to every description.

**Publishing rhythm:** one 3–5 minute Story Seed plus three Shorts a week for 12 weeks. Each Story Seed produces two related Shorts, but do not simply crop a horizontal video; edit a native 9:16 version with larger text and a stronger first second.

**Titles that serve a parent search:**

- `A Gentle Before-Meal Sunnah for Children | Sunnah Seed`
- `Bedtime Du‘a for Little Muslims | A Calm Family Routine`
- `How to Teach Kindness Through Everyday Sunnah | For Parents & Children`

**Made-for-kids decision:** make the decision per item with legal/platform guidance. Clearly child-directed episodes should be accurately designated. Since comments, end screens, and notification features can be limited, say a simple verbal CTA such as “Grown-ups, find the next Seed in our playlists or description” and use parent-facing companion posts to invite feedback.

### 5.2 Instagram — discovery, saves, and daily relationship

**Purpose:** earn repeat exposure with busy caregivers and give them posts worth saving or sharing.

**Profile setup**

- Professional account, connected to the Facebook Page.
- Display name uses the full brand term: `Sunnah Seed | Islamic Kids` (subject to character limits).
- Add accessible alt text, captions/subtitles, website link, public business email, and clear parent-facing bio.
- Pin three posts: **Start Here**, **How we check our sources**, **Free Family Starter Guide**.
- Highlights: `Start Here`, `Sunnahs`, `Du‘as`, `Stories`, `For Parents`, `Printables`, `About`.

**Weekly cadence:** cross-post the three vertical videos as Reels; publish one native parent carousel; share 3–5 light Story frames across the week (polls/questions aimed at adults, a printable reminder, a behind-the-scenes script check). Use Stories for conversation, not child data collection.

**Creative rules**

- First frame states the benefit in under seven words: “A gentle bedtime Sunnah” or “Try this at snack time.”
- Captions stay readable with sound off; meaningful text never sits behind interface controls.
- Use a cover title so a parent can scan the grid in five seconds.
- Aim CTAs at adult actions: save, share with a caregiver, download a parent printable, watch the full story. Avoid asking children to comment.

### 5.3 Facebook — community trust and partner reach

**Purpose:** reach parents, grandparents, local family groups, teachers, and masjid communities that share helpful resources.

- Replace the generic share link with a memorable Page username and use it consistently.
- Cross-post Reels, then adapt the opening copy: Facebook parents often value the context, source note, and printable link more than a very short caption.
- Publish one weekly text/photo/carousel-style parent post: a reflection question, activity instructions, or a behind-the-scenes source-review note.
- Use albums for **Printable activities**, **Parent guides**, and **Seasonal resources**.
- Invite distribution partners to share a pre-written caption and link rather than giving them edit access.
- Do not start a large child-facing group. If a parent community is later needed, it must have explicit rules, active adult moderation, no child posts, and safeguarding escalation procedures.

### 5.4 Expansion only after consistency

Reserve handles on relevant platforms now, but do not spread the team thin. Evaluate TikTok, Pinterest, email, a website, or a podcast **after 12 consistent weeks** and only where the child-safety, source-review, and moderation processes can be maintained. A simple parent-owned email list is more valuable than chasing a fourth algorithm.

### Phase 1 — consistent launch (Days 15–42)

- Publish on the promised schedule; never publish an unreviewed religious claim merely to fill the calendar.
- Announce the mission through founder, trusted families, local teachers, masjid family programmes, and Muslim parenting partners. Ask partners to **share**, not merely “follow.”
- Reply to adult-safe comments/messages within one business day; compile repeated parent questions for the Parent Patch series.
- Create one free, genuinely useful lead resource: **“Seven Tiny Sunnah Moments for a Gentle Family Week”**—reviewed, printable, and emailed only to adults who opt in.
- Hold a 30-minute weekly review: content published, questions received, source/correction issues, and top saved/shared post.

### Phase 2 — learn and refine (Days 43–70)

- Identify the top 20% of posts by **saves/shares per reach**, not just raw views; make two follow-up episodes for each winning topic.
- Test one variable per week: hook wording, cover title, posting time, length, parent question, or carousel first slide. Keep topic and quality constant where possible.
- Start a small partner pilot (3–5 trusted educators/parent creators). Give a source card and clear disclosure/usage expectations.
- If a modest budget is available, promote only parent-facing resources to adults after privacy/policy review. Do not base targeting on children, collect child data, or use a child-directed video as an ad funnel.

### Phase 3 — build proof and plan the next quarter (Days 71–90)

- Produce a 90-day report: output, quality-control rate, learning topics, audience signals, partner results, and safeguarding incidents/corrections.
- Refresh the next 12-week editorial calendar around proven topics and one upcoming seasonal moment only if it can be prepared and reviewed well.
- Ask 5–10 adult followers/teachers for structured feedback: What did a child repeat? What did they practise? What was unclear? What would they share?
- Decide whether to scale long-form video, build email/website resources, offer a school/masjid toolkit, or test a small paid parent campaign. Do not make this decision on follower count alone.

## 7. Operating system: people, workflow, and budget

### 7.1 Lean founding team

| Responsibility | Minimum role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mission, partnerships, final approval | Founder/brand lead | Owns voice and operational accountability; not the sole source validator unless qualified. |
| Script research and source register | Researcher/content lead | Prepares source packets before creative production. |

One person can hold several roles at launch, but **research/review must not be skipped** because the editor is under pressure.

### 7.2 Asset workflow

`Topic backlog → source packet → lesson objective → script → Islamic review → design/edit → accessibility/source check → schedule → publish → moderate → weekly learning review → archive/update`

Use a shared tracker with these columns:

```text
Asset ID | Series | Topic | Audience | Source links | Grading/notes | Reviewer | Script approved | Visual policy checked | Consent/licence checked | Platform | Audience setting | Scheduled date | URL | 7/30-day results | Corrections
```

### 7.3 Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] One child-appropriate learning objective, in one sentence.
- [ ] Source is in the register; citation, grading, and translation are checked.
- [ ] Qualified reviewer approval is recorded.
- [ ] No questionable sacred depictions, medical promises, shaming language, or misleading simplification.
- [ ] Parent-facing CTA; no request for child contact/personal information.
- [ ] Captions, readable text contrast, alt text/description, and source note are present.
- [ ] Music/audio, images, fonts, and footage have usage rights and comply with the agreed policy.
- [ ] Correct channel audience/age setting and moderation rules are applied.
- [ ] A correction contact is available.

### 7.4 Investment guidance

Start lean and spend on quality control rather than vanity promotion.

| Tier | What to fund first | Indicative approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Essential** | Reviewer time, basic microphone/light, caption/editing workflow, templates, secure account management. | Produce fewer, better episodes consistently. |
| **Growth** | Freelance editor/designer, free printable design, small parent-only promotion, partner toolkit. | Add only when the weekly system is stable. |
| **Scale** | Producer, website/email platform, safeguarding/legal consultation, translation/localisation, educator partnerships. | Scale topics that have proven useful and reviewable. |

A paid campaign should have a single adult-oriented objective (for example, printable downloads or qualified parent video views), a privacy review, a capped test budget, and a documented stop rule. Organic usefulness and trusted partner shares are the first growth engine.

## 8. Measurement that respects the mission

### 8.1 Scorecard

Record every platform separately; a “view” is not comparable across platforms. Measure weekly and review monthly.

| Goal | Leading indicators | Quality indicator | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reliable delivery** | Planned vs published; approval completion; turnaround time. | 100% of published teaching claims have a source record and review. | Is the team ready to increase cadence? |
| **Useful discovery** | Reach, non-follower reach, profile visits, YouTube impressions. | Saves + shares ÷ reach; parent link clicks. | Which hook/topic deserves a sequel? |
| **Learning attention** | Watch time, average percentage viewed, Shorts completion/rewatch. | Strong retention after the first learning point, not just an attention-grabbing first second. | Is the format clear and well paced? |
| **Parent trust** | Positive adult questions, printable downloads, email sign-ups, partner shares. | Comments that describe a family practice, not only emojis. | Which resources deepen the relationship? |
| **Safety and integrity** | Moderation response time, issues escalated, corrections. | Zero unreviewed claims or preventable privacy breaches. | What policy/training must improve? |

**Define a simple quality engagement rate:**

`(saves + shares + substantive adult comments + link clicks) ÷ reach × 100`

Keep reactions/likes in the dashboard, but do not let them decide the strategy alone.

### 8.2 Targets without false precision

There is no usable baseline in this public audit, so follower targets would be arbitrary. Use the first four publishing weeks to establish a baseline, then set percentage improvement goals for Weeks 5–12:

- **Output:** 90%+ of planned weekly assets published; no rushed, unreviewed religious content.
- **Trust:** 100% source/reviewer completion; all safety incidents logged and addressed within the written process.
- **Utility:** increase save/share rate and qualified parent actions by **20%** over the Weeks 1–4 baseline on the same content formats.
- **Attention:** improve median retention/watch time by **15%** from the baseline through clearer hooks and pacing, not sensationalism.
- **Community:** secure 3–5 trusted partner shares or testimonials by Day 90 and use them only with permission.

At Day 30, the owner should insert real counts (followers, reach, watch time, saves, shares, downloads) and convert these relative targets into realistic next-quarter numeric goals.

## 9. Community, partnerships, and response scripts

### 9.1 Engagement rules

- Address the adult: “Thank you for sharing this with your family.”
- Invite reflection, not child disclosure: “Which part of the routine works best in your home?”
- Answer simple source requests with the reviewed reference; route rulings and personal cases to the reviewer/local scholar.
- Do not debate in comment threads. Correct calmly once with a source; hide/restrict abusive or unsafe comments; preserve evidence where policy requires it.
- Announce corrections clearly: “We updated the reference in this post. Jazakum Allahu khayran to the parent who helped us check it.”

### 9.2 Safe reply templates

**Source request**

> JazakAllahu khayran for checking. Our reference for this lesson is [reference]. Our content is educational and reviewed before publishing; for a personal ruling, please speak with a qualified local scholar you trust.

**Personal child/family question**

> Thank you for trusting us. We cannot give individual religious, medical, or safeguarding advice in messages. A qualified local scholar/appropriate professional who knows your circumstances can guide you best.

**Correction**

> JazakAllahu khayran for flagging this. We are checking the source with our reviewer and have paused the asset while we do so. Accuracy matters to us.

### 9.3 Partnership ladder

1. **Listen:** make a list of 25 aligned local/global parent educators, masjid family teams, Islamic schools, children’s bookshops, and child-safe creators.
2. **Give value first:** send a reviewed printable/episode and a one-page source card, without demanding a post.
3. **Co-create carefully:** a parent workshop, a Ramadan/Eid resource, or a sourced activity pack—only after safeguarding and review expectations are agreed in writing.
4. **Measure quality:** track partner link clicks, saves, printable use, and adult feedback—not just reach.

Never pay for or accept a partnership that asks Sunnah Seed to compromise sources, child privacy, respectful visual policy, or disclosure standards.
